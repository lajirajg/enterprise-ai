# Business outcomes

Status: **In progress** — outcome framing and measurement template available.

AI investment starts with a change in business performance, an accountable owner and a baseline. A model benchmark or token count is not itself business value.

| Outcome | Example metric | Guardrail |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Customer experience | Time to accepted resolution | Repeat contacts and complaint rate |
| Productivity and cost | Cost per completed case | Quality and human review effort |
| Revenue and growth | Conversion or retention change | Customer suitability and consent |
| Risk and resilience | Loss events or recovery time | Control exceptions and false positives |
| Employee experience | Time spent on avoidable rework | Workload and oversight fatigue |
| Innovation | Time from hypothesis to validated service | Experiment cost and operational readiness |

## Outcome brief

For each use case record: outcome owner; affected process and population; baseline period; target; metric definition; quality and risk guardrails; data source; implementation cost; ongoing operating cost; evaluation design; review date; and stop/scale criteria.

Use a controlled comparison where feasible. Track adoption and process changes so improvement is not automatically attributed to AI. Include failure handling and review costs in the economic case.

## Traceability

Outcome → business process → operating capabilities → platform and digital dependencies → evidence → measured result.

For an invoice exception pilot, compare accepted resolutions per reviewer hour and end-to-end cycle time. Guard against incorrect postings and displaced work. Map the pilot to [orchestration](../architecture/layers/intelligent-orchestration/index.md), [execution](../architecture/layers/execution/index.md) and [continuous improvement](../architecture/layers/continuous-improvement/index.md).
